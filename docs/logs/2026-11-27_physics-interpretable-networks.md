# Physics-interpretable joint networks, 27 November 2026

The [experimental plan](../ONGOING_PHYSICS_INTERPRETABLE_NETWORKS_2026-11-27.md) was committed as `40519fc` before these measurements. The strong baseline is the June width-152, three-block joint checkpoint. Its matched reconstruction-only control used the same training split and disabled the event loss. This round covers four experiments on that model family. [Numerical results and diagnostics](../results/2026-11-27/README.md).

## What the interventions established

Increasing an ambiguous large jet's mass toward the W mass increased its W-versus-alternatives logit margin in both networks. In the joint network the full edit increased the margin by 1.63, changed the winning jet in 8.99% of selected events and flipped the leptonic verdict in 3.63%. A higher tag value from a kinematically matched real jet reduced the W margin by 0.75, yet raised the event signal probability by 2.12 percentage points. A neutral tag replacement produced a mean change near zero. These are controlled model responses on a selected population. A mass or tag edit does not generate a new collision with a known label.

Restoring block-1 head-0's clean contribution in the joint model recovered 36.0% of the mean object-probability error when the W proxy rotated toward the Higgs proxy and 23.6% when it rotated away. Another head recovered more in the away direction, and the matched reconstruction-only network depended more on block-1 head-3. A head index across two trained models need not denote the same computation. The edit changes other relative angles and the jet's absolute azimuth as well. This is evidence of a causal contribution to the output response, with incomplete specificity for ΔR(h,W). [All doses, control heads, sample definitions and signed yields](2026-11-27_jet-intervention-results.md).

## What compression retained

At the matched 1,600-update budget, labels plus soft predictions from the frozen joint network improved all-event AUC by 0.0066–0.0105 and signed perfect reconstruction by 0.87–1.06 percentage points versus labels alone across three paired width-48 student seeds on the expanded confirmation sample. Event argmax accuracy was lower with distillation in the initial comparison. All six students lost reconstruction accuracy relative to the teacher, especially in the resolved topologies. Signs of continuing learning at the endpoint limit conclusions about ultimate capacity.

One distilled scalar had R² = 0.953 against its fitted formula and retained 94.6% whole-assignment agreement after in-network replacement. The fitted scalar had zero response to jet-mass edits while the original scalar moved. The downstream W probability could still respond through other paths even with the scalar replaced by a constant. Ordinary agreement therefore substantially overstates how much of the mechanism this formula explains. [Six training arms, formula substitutions, identity/mean controls and larger tail evaluation](2026-11-27_compact-student-results.md).

## Classification from reconstructed quantities

The physics readout used 29 candidate features and indicators derived from predicted assignments on both signal and background. On reconstruction-valid confirmation events, mean AUC was 0.97529 with the physics MLP, 0.97530 after quadrupling its parameters, and 0.98630 for the native head. A simple linear refit on pooled internal features gave 0.98667. Adding physics inputs to the linear refit gave 0.98673. The nonlinear pooled and combined fits reached 0.98021 and 0.97998. The wider physics control and pooled linear control make it unlikely that simply widening this readout recovers the missing discrimination. The comparable linear scores do not imply that a short physics formula for the pooled representation has been found.

The larger proportional signal deficit of the physics MLP occurred among ambiguous assignments. Most absolute signal loss came from the more numerous high-margin events. Individual low-mass bins have substantial signed-weight uncertainty. See the [pilot, three-seed comparisons, per-mass yields, and realised background](2026-11-27_physics-readout-results.md).

## Azimuthal symmetry

The frozen audit found changes in whole-event reconstruction in 6.21%, 7.74%, 8.47% and 7.49% of signal events for global rotations of +45°, +90°, 180°, and −90°. The identity edit was exactly zero. Every measured nonzero angle crossed the plan's 1% trigger. The three-way d152 experiment used ordinary absolute azimuth, random global rotations during training, and lepton-relative sine and cosine. All arms started from one trained checkpoint, so the seeds replicate fine-tuning randomness. The relative arm reused the absolute-angle input column for sine and initialised the cosine column to zero; the comparison includes that adaptation cost.

The [rotation results](2026-11-27_rotation-results.md) give the paired seeds, reference checkpoint, six reconstruction categories, ten mass points per channel, realised background and a post-hoc four-orientation average of the frozen model. The average was added after observing the initial fine-tuning performance; it is an inference-time control with four forward passes.

## Evaluation and learnings

- Within each simulated dataset (DSID), the frozen models trained on odd row positions. Independent row residues were used for downstream fitting, threshold selection and evaluation. IDs are (DSID, row). Distinct rows can carry repeated physical inputs. See the duplicate audit below.
- Report signed Monte Carlo perfect reconstruction by all six truth topologies. For search-facing comparisons calibrate to 200 expected background **per reconstructed channel** on a separate sample, freeze the cut, and give actual assessment background with its Monte Carlo uncertainty. Each mass hypothesis is a separate search. A sum of yields across mass hypotheses has no interpretation as a physical event yield.
- AUC uses nonnegative absolute weights. The readout comparison above is restricted to reconstruction-valid events; the student and rotation training comparisons use all events. Every November arm uses the same predicted-reconstruction validity and channel rule within its comparison. June's event-head channel assignment and normalisation differ.
- The small pilots gave unstable tail estimates; the student and readout comparisons were repeated on common larger samples with 355,000 background events per split. Even there the calibration uncertainty is additional to the tabulated square root of summed squared weights. An AUC or a signal-yield gain alone does not establish improved expected limits.
- On the local Apple GPU the bounded training benchmark measured roughly 12,200 events/s at batch 512 and 21,900 at 1,024. Batch 128 measured 842 events/s including the first-call startup cost. We selected 1,024. An input-variable name error stopped the relative arm before its first update. After fixing it and verifying both head outputs under a test rotation, we resumed from the two completed arm summaries.
- Software checks passed for both new rotation/threshold regressions, five dual-head compatibility tests, seventeen joint-primitives tests, eight training-mode smoke tests, and the single-head fitted-readout integration test. The two integration suites ran on private 8,192-row-per-signal-dataset fixtures. The scientific studies used the original full-size memmaps read-only.

## Duplicate audit

A scan of all 23,295,355 source rows across all 49 nonempty datasets found 8,075 pairs with identical model inputs. They were confined to two simulated background samples (700323 and 700327). Pair verification found identical complete stored rows, including weights, so their underlying origin requires upstream provenance. There were 5,773 distinct even rows with twins in the odd-row teacher training set. No identical signal rows or cross-dataset pairs were found. The row split therefore provides imperfect isolation for background. An input-grouped split, with duplicate and signed-weight bookkeeping verified before training, would give a cleaner next-generation baseline.

For the large readout/student samples, the conservative union of duplicate exclusions removes 240 calibration and 273 confirmation background rows. No student or native confirmation selection changed after removal and recalibration. At fixed thresholds the maximum absolute contribution to any learned readout’s retained confirmation background was 0.025 expected events. In the three-way rotation study, the maximum direct contribution reported for a passing duplicate was 0.019 expected events. Removing six ambiguous-event duplicate rows changed the intervention mean margins by less than 0.00013 logits; the geometry cohort had none. These checks address exact equality and selection contamination. They do not identify near-duplicates or establish the physical cross-section interpretation of repeated source records. ## What I would retain

Keep the trained width-152 model as the performance anchor. Across the three paired seeds, random rotation augmentation outperformed ordinary fine-tuning on reconstruction and AUC. All nine short fine-tunes lost nominal reconstruction relative to the frozen checkpoint. Lepton-relative encoding reduced the mean signal assignment flip rate to 0.000–0.001% across seeds, with a reconstruction cost under the tested warm start. Two seeds had zero flips. For seed 2703 one signal event changed assignment at +45°.

The post-hoc four-orientation average improved the frozen model from 88.58% to 89.12% perfect reconstruction on the 30,000-signal comparison. On a separate 136,039-event signal confirmation sample it improved from 88.445% to 89.171%, a signed paired difference of **+0.726 percentage points** (95% interval +0.489 to +0.963). Absolute improvement estimates were positive at all ten masses and in all six topologies, with some individual intervals including zero. It costs four forward passes. It is exactly invariant to the discrete quarter-turn group up to floating-point effects; orientation sensitivity remains between those angles. The observed AUC improvement on the mixed sample and the separate signal-only confirmation do not establish an improvement in expected limits.
